<p>When Patrik Jaroš first sketched the idea for MindBites, the brief fit on a napkin: <strong>clean energy, calm focus, and a dose you control.</strong> Turning that brief into a gummy took eighteen months, 47 formula iterations and more lab reports than we'd like to count.</p>

<h2>Why a gummy is harder than it looks</h2>
<p>Putting active ingredients into a capsule is easy. Putting them into a chew that tastes good, holds its shape and keeps the same dose in every piece is not. Caffeine is bitter, tyrosine is hard to dissolve and heat during production can affect sensitive actives. Every change to one variable moved three others.</p>

<h2>The iteration loop</h2>
<p>Each prototype went through the same loop:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Formulate:</strong> adjust actives, sweeteners, acids and gelling agents.</li>
  <li><strong>Produce:</strong> run a small pilot batch.</li>
  <li><strong>Analyse:</strong> measure the active content per gummy and how evenly it is spread across the batch.</li>
  <li><strong>Taste:</strong> blind tastings with a small panel, scored on flavour, bitterness and texture.</li>
  <li><strong>Store:</strong> keep samples under controlled conditions and test again weeks later.</li>
</ul>
<p>Most prototypes failed at least one step. Iteration 31 tasted great but drifted on dose. Iteration 38 was spot on for dose but too firm to chew. Iteration 47 passed everything.</p>

<h2>What "done" means to us</h2>
<p>We called the formula finished when three things were true: every gummy delivered the target dose within tight tolerances, the taste held up through shelf life, and the gummy was something people actually wanted to reach for. That's the bar every batch still has to clear today.</p>
